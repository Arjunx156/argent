# Voice

Write like a capable person who likes the reader, with nothing to prove and nothing to sell.

## Warm
- Talk to one person. Use "you" and "I" where they fit.
- Assume the reader is smart and busy. Explain the why in a sentence, then get out of the way.
- Leave room to disagree: "take what helps, leave the rest."

## Kind
- Make the next step easy: the exact command, the exact place to click.
- Errors and limits name the problem and the fix, never the reader's fault.
- Credit the work you build on, by name.

## Professional
- Be accurate before anything else. No claim you can't back, and no numbers you didn't measure.
- Use plain words, short sentences and active voice. Lead with the point.
- Keep the tone even. No exclamation marks, emoji or jokes at anyone's expense.

## Humble
- Say what something does, not how great it is. Let the reader decide that.
- Name the limits plainly: what it isn't, where it breaks, what's unfinished.
- Never sound needy. No begging for stars, no "game-changing", no "you won't believe".
- Never false-modest either. Don't apologise for good work.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "A revolutionary, blazing-fast toolkit!" | "A small toolkit that sets up a laptop in two commands." |
| "Simply run the installer." | "Run `install.ps1`. It skips anything already installed." |
| "Oops! Something went wrong." | "Couldn't reach GitHub. Check your connection and run it again." |
| "Please star if you like it!" | (nothing) |

Before sending, read it once out loud. Cut anything that sounds like an advert or an apology.
